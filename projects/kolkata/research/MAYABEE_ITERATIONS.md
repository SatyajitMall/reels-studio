## The opening
**What makes the reference's opening work** (`analysis/references/watch/sheet_01.jpg`):
- a person does something (a man at a record stall puts a record on);
- that action is why the music starts;
- the title lands on the close detail at 3.9 s.

**What we keep:** a person or a motivated action in shot 1, the music has a reason to start, the title on a musical hit.
**What we don't copy:** the record device.

**How these were made:**
- the same best-of pool for all four, every cut on Mayabee's beats (`tools/audition_edl.py --pool v3`);
- each iteration uses a different part of the song, with its own opening;
- colour is the camera's (offline preview);
- landmark times are from our copy of the song (the music video's audio).

## Which opening is strongest, by the reference's test
| | Person or action in shot 1 | Why the music starts | Title on a hit |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | ✅ the chai-wallah stirring | the city wakes; the song's first note | ✅ first strong beat, in the steam |
| M2 | ◐ a boat's bow moving, no face | arriving home by river | ✅ the drop |
| M3 | ✅ dhakis and dancers | Pujo, the reason you come back | ✅ the hook after the breath |
| M4 | ◐ people walking away from camera | crossing into the city | ✅ an accent |

**My pick: M3 or M1.**
- **M3** has the strongest first second (Pujo motion, colour and people) and the most earned title hit (the breath, then the hook).
- **M1** is the purest echo of the reference's logic (a person's action starts the song) without copying it, and Instagram's start is simply 0:00.
- **M4** is the only one that ends with the song itself.
