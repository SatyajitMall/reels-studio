# Stage 5 long-list: Bengali songs for "A Bengali far from home" (KOLKATA round 3, 2026-11-06)

Written against `criteria.md` (6-beat spine, mood-only matching, 65–110 BPM or half-time, sparse vocal, one 40–60 s continuous section) and Stage 2 of `SONG_RESEARCH_METHOD.md` (what the pool can show).

**How to read this**
- Every song below was checked against at least one source (links in each row; full list at the end). Songs we couldn't confirm were left out or moved to "Checked and set aside".
- **What it's about** is our own summary, from the sources, of what the song means. No lyrics are quoted. Where no source describes the lyric, the row says so: listen before shortlisting.
- **Feel.** No source gave a BPM for any of these songs. A † marks details from a source. Everything else (slow / medium) is our estimate, to be measured with `tools/song_fit.py` in Stage 6.
- **IG library.** No source showed reels using any of these as Instagram library audio, so every row says *unknown*. The director checks the top 8 in the app (the gate). Labels are given where a source names one.
- **Beats:** 1 river morning · 2 the roads · 3 where we gathered · 4 evening play · 5 Pujo calls you back · 6 night, the ache to return.
- Excluded by instruction: "Jaane Woh Kaise Log The" (*Pyaasa*) and "Amar Shohor" (Chandrabindoo / Shreya Ghoshal).

## Ranked top 8 for this story

Before scoring, the director checks these eight in Instagram's audio picker (the gate).

1. **Agomonir Gaan** (Anupam Roy, 2019): the only song here written *for* Bengalis abroad who miss home at Pujo. Its arc (a call → the festival → home) is our spine, and Pujo is our strongest night footage.
2. **Ghore Pherar Gaan** (Moheener Ghoraguli / Chandrani Banerjee, 1999): the band-era song of going home for people living away. River and memory, with the bittersweet sense that home can't be had again. Its availability needs checking.
3. **Purano Sei Diner Kotha** (Tagore): old days, a parting and a hoped-for reunion, known to every Bengali. Choose the recording by its 45 s arc; the 2023 film version is modern and short (2:44).
4. **Coffee Houser Sei Addata** (Manna Dey, 1983): the Kolkata memory song, with our Coffee House and College Street footage. Use the closing idea that the adda goes on for the hope lift. Pick either #3 or #4: they share the Auld Lang Syne melody.
5. **Ore Neel Doriya** (Abdul Jabbar, 1978): a sailor's ache to go home, with train and water built into the arrangement. Matches our river and roads, and is known across both Bengals.
6. **Abar Bochhor Koori Pore** (Rupankar Bagchi, 2022): Kolkatans living in Bombay made it about coming back to the city and old friends, with a modern production. Lower recognition.
7. **O Nodi Re** (Hemanta, 1959): Hemanta sings to a river, in a film about a man far from home in Calcutta. It suits our river shots and blue-hour Howrah, if the lyric checks out and the old recording holds up.
8. **Apur Paayer Chhaap** (Arijit Singh, 2014): tender, folk-tinged nostalgia in a voice the young diaspora knows. Warm, not tragic. The lyric is still to be checked.

Next in line: *Jotne Rakha* (homecoming warmth at Pujo, but new), *Tomake Chai* (a love song to Calcutta that reads as romance first) and *Gram Chhara* (beat 2 only, a song of leaving).

## Checked and set aside

- **"Kolkata"** (Anupam Roy & Shreya Ghoshal, *Praktan*, 2016): reviewers call it happy, upbeat and laidback, like a city tourism video. That repeats v2's cheerful-catalogue failure. [musicaloud review](https://musicaloud.com/2016/07/16/praktan-music-review-bengali-soundtrack) · [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_songs_about_Kolkata)
- **Nachiketa, "Kolkata"** (*Ei Besh Bhalo Achhi*, 1993): it exists, but no source we could reach says what it's about or how it sounds. Listen before considering it. [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_songs_about_Kolkata)
- **Moheener Ghoraguli's Kolkata songs.** None fits as well as #2:
  - "Kolkata Kolkata" (1977 EP *Shangbigno Pakhikul O Kolkata Bishayak*) is an ode to urban life in a jazz/prog style ([northsound](https://northsound.fm/album/shangbigno-pakhikul-o-kolkata-bishayak-201777)). One writer hears a Moheener song with the city as its refrain as gritty and desperate ([substack](https://angstential.substack.com/p/hometowns-and-the-poetics-of-never)).
  - "Shohorer Ushnotomo Dine" (Gorer Math, for the Moheener project) is popular but its theme isn't described ([t2online](https://t2online.in/music/indian/a-night-where-moheener-ghoraguli-breathed-again-at-skinny-mo-s-jazz-club/2006062)).
  - "Tomay Dilam" is a love song some use for the city ([t2online](https://t2online.in/screen/bengali/debopriyo-mukherjee-writes-about-shohorer-ushnotomo-dine/485089)).
  - "Prithibi ta naki chhoto hote hote" is about a shrinking world, not home.
- **Kabir Suman, "Probashi Bangalir Gaan"** ("the expatriate Bengali's song"): on our exact subject, but satirical and self-critical, the wrong tone. [sumanami.co.uk](https://sumanami.co.uk/%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%80-%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%99%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%97%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B0-%E0%A6%97%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8/)
- **"Abar Ashibo Phire"** (Jibanananda Das's "I shall return to Bengal" poem): the perfect text, but we found only recitations (e.g. Pradip Ghosh, 2003), no sung version. Possible as a spoken-word idea. [JioSaavn](https://www.jiosaavn.com/song/abar-aasibo-phire-recitation/Chladh1Ac0M)
- **Bhupen Hazarika, "Ami Ek Jajabor"**: a wanderer for whom the world is home, the opposite of longing. [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/features/bhupen-hazarika-assam-centenary-brahmaputra-singer-artists-jajabor-ganga-206605)
- **"O Kolkata"** (Shreya Ghoshal, *Uraan*, 2020; Joy Sarkar): an ode to the city, but no source describes its mood. [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_songs_about_Kolkata)
- **"Pherari Mon"** (Shreya Ghoshal & Babul Supriyo, *Antaheen*, 2009; National Award for lyrics and singing): a romantic drama; no source ties it to home or nostalgia. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antaheen)
- **Bhoomi, "Barandaye Roddur"** (1999): about waiting for someone who never comes; danceable. [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/art-entertainment/bhoomi-is-b-flat-news-231729)
- **"Bajlo Tomar Alor Benu"** (Supriti Ghosh, *Mahisasuramardini*; Saregama holds the rights): the Pujo-dawn anthem. Devotional and joyous, so a beat-5 insert at most, not the bed. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahisasuramardini_(radio_programme))
- **"Phire Cholo Apon Ghore"** (K. C. Dey, *Chandidas*, 1934): a homecoming feel, but the recording is from 1934. [BengalInfo](https://bengalinfo.com/gaan.php?id=292)
- ***033*** (2010, music by Chandrabindoo, lyrics by Srijato): a film about young people leaving Kolkata, but its known song, "Onnyo Kothao Chol" ("let's go somewhere else"), points the wrong way and its theme isn't verified. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/033)
- **Fossils, "Aaro Ekbar"** (2002) and **"Phire Cholo"** (2009): their themes aren't verified. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossils_(band))
- **Kishore / Lata / Sandhya in Bengali**: we found no verified song of theirs about home, *probash* or return; the classic catalogue we checked is romantic. Sandhya is in the list through #18, and a Kishore recording of #3 exists.
